I distinctly remember the day when I was introduced to a person that would be perhaps the single greatest career challenge I have had in nearly 25 years.

I had been the manager of a team of data science and ML engineers for a few months, and the boss called us out to some unpleasant mid-Western city as it was both the home of this creature, and half way between our New York base and San Francisco. 

"I think we should really move Bob's team under Chase" - that was literally the first words I'd overheard this person saying in a work environment. He was less than a week into his new role. He knew neither Bob nor Chase, nor their teams, nor the work they did - but that didn't stop him making a recommendation that might affect a few dozen people's live. A whim, something to say as one does watching a ballgame, a merely trival sentence uttered without thought - a thoughtless, careless exhortation, except one that made him look like someone with gravitas, command, even intelligence. This was to be my first introduction to Lehi.

This fucker was a thorn in my side for nearly three years, and every utterance, meeting, email, and DM this absolute cnut put out was to serve a single purpose - to grow and metastasize across the org until their ego and craving for power over others was satisfied. 

I don't know exactly when it dawned on me this fucker had it in for me. One obvious data point was our common boss, a VP, who let me know weekly how this asshole was complaining about my team, not from the "doing bad work" viewpoint but more from the "these are resource who should report to me" worldview. 

This cnut would go and setup weekly recurring 1:1's with my team, and tell them to prioritise tasks, setting up recurring meetings with the team and wanting influence over my team's roadmap. 


One of the very last interactions I had with Lehi was telling - we were in a Zoom meeting where the third person never showed and cancelled a few minutes into the call. He pivoted and asked, repeatedly, for documentation describing what I was working on. Not the actual information, but shared documentation. I found this particularly weird as someone genuinely interested and understanding of the work would simply ask - this guy wanted written proof.
